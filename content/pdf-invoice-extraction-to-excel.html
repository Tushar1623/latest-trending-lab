  • Best for small businesses: At 5 minutes per invoice and $10 an hour, manual entry costs about $0.83 per invoice, while extraction with a $9 Make Core plan and about $0.01 of model usage per invoice puts breakeven near 11 invoices a month. At 300 invoices the calculator gives $250 manual against $12 automated, and at 20 invoices $16.67 against $9.20. Automate invoices with a consistent layout first, where extraction is most reliable.
  • Best for accountants and agencies: Keep a review step for amounts, dates and tax fields before anything reaches the books, and have the workflow check that line items sum to the subtotal and subtotal plus tax equals the total. Mismatches go to a person. Make Core's 10,000 credits cover roughly 1,600 invoices a month at six modules each, so volume is rarely the constraint, accuracy is.
  • Pricing trap: Extraction is never perfect, and one wrong figure can cost more than the time you saved. The $0.01 per invoice assumes clean text. Poor scans need OCR first, long invoices use more tokens, and review time is not in the tool cost. Measure the error rate on your own invoices before you trust a saving, and protect sensitive information when you send documents to an AI service.

How the numbers are calculated

The default scenario is 300 invoices a month, 5 minutes of manual entry per invoice and $10 an hour of staff time. Manual cost is invoices times minutes divided by 60 times hourly cost: 300 times 5 divided by 60 times $10, or $250. The automated workflow is a $9 Make Core plan plus about $0.01 per invoice of model usage. That figure comes from published rates: an invoice of about 2,000 input tokens and 500 output tokens on Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $2 and $10 per million costs $0.004 plus $0.005, or $0.009. So 300 invoices cost $9 plus $3, or $12. Breakeven is $9 divided by the per-invoice saving of $0.823, about 11 invoices. At 20 invoices manual cost is $16.67 and automation $9.20. Review time and error correction are not included, and a 2 percent error rate with 10 minutes of fixing per error would add 60 minutes a month at 300 invoices, so measure it. We have not measured extraction accuracy on real invoices, and results depend on layout and scan quality. Run 30 of your own invoices through the workflow, count the errors, and enter the corrected time per invoice in the calculator. Because the largest inputs here are assumptions, time one real task from start to finish, record the minutes and the tool cost, and enter those figures. Re-run the calculator after your first month of actual use and note the date, so you can see how far the estimate drifted from reality.
