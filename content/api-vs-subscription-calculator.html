Direct answer

  • Best for solo builders: At one seat and medium context, about 3,000 prompt tokens per request, pay-as-you-go use of Claude Sonnet 5.5 or GPT-6.1 Sol costs about $0.019 per 1,000 words generated. That keeps the API under a $20 ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro seat until roughly 1.0 million words a month. Heavy RAG context of about 15,000 tokens per request moves the breakeven down to roughly 460,000 words. If you mostly chat, upload files and use the apps, stay on the flat plan.
  • Best for teams and agencies: Each extra seat adds $20 on ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro, and $25 on ChatGPT Business or Claude Team standard seats billed monthly ($20 annual, two-seat minimum). Five seats cost $100 a month, which medium-context API use only reaches at about 5.2 million words. The catch is the interface: someone must build and maintain it, so count those hours before you declare savings.
  • Pricing trap: Subscriptions are flat but capped. Claude Team, for example, applies a rolling five-hour window plus weekly limits, so heavy days can be throttled. API bills have no natural cap, and retries, long pasted context and agent loops multiply tokens. Set a spending limit in the vendor console and log tokens per task for the first month before you commit.

How the numbers are calculated

The default scenario is one seat producing 300,000 words a month, split into 300 requests of about 1,000 words each, with medium context of 3,000 prompt tokens per request. Output tokens are 300,000 times about 1.333, or roughly 399,900, which at $10 per million is $4.00. Input tokens are 300 requests times 3,000, or 900,000, which at $2 per million is $1.80. The Claude Sonnet 5.5 or GPT-6.1 Sol total is $5.80, against $20 for one subscription seat, a saving of $14.20. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at $0.30 and $1.20 comes to $0.75. At the slider maximum of 2 million words with heavy context the API total is about $86.66, so the flat plan wins there. Breakeven per seat is $20 divided by the cost per 1,000 words: about 1.40 million words with small context, 1.03 million with medium and 462,000 with heavy. This is arithmetic on published rates, not a measurement. We have not timed responses, and subscription limits change often, so check them before relying on the plan. To test this on your own numbers, run ten real requests, read the billed input and output tokens in the provider's usage dashboard, and compare them with the assumed token counts. If the real tokens differ by more than about 10 percent, change the assumption in Lab Settings, because token counts drive almost the whole result.
