Direct answer

  • Best for solo builders: Claude Sonnet 5.5 and OpenAI GPT-6.1 Sol both list $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, so a 1,500-word article with a 1,500-token prompt costs about 2.3 cents on either. That is roughly $4.60 for 200 articles a month, and about 870 articles of that size cost $20, the price of one subscription seat. Draft in bulk with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, which costs about $0.57 for the same 200 articles at its peak rate, and keep a premium model for the pieces you edit most.
  • Best for agencies: Run a 20-article pilot on each model, then price the winner at volume. At 1,000 articles a month the same assumptions give about $23 on Claude Sonnet 5.5 or GPT-6.1 Sol, $23.74 on the older GPT-4o and $2.85 on DeepSeek. The gap between premium models is small next to editing time, so score each model on the minutes a draft needs before it is publishable, not only on cost per article.
  • Pricing trap: Input tokens are billed on every request. A 10,000-token style guide sent with each article adds about $0.02 at $2 per million, almost doubling the 2.3-cent base cost. Prompt caching and batch processing, which the major providers discount by up to 50 percent for non-urgent work, are the usual fixes. One 2026 pricing guide also reports that newer Claude models count about 30 percent more tokens for the same text than Sonnet 4.6 and earlier, so measure your own text.

How the numbers are calculated

The default scenario is 200 articles a month at 1,500 words each, with a 1,500-token prompt per article. Output tokens equal words times about 1.333, so one article produces roughly 2,000 output tokens. Cost per article is input tokens times the input rate plus output tokens times the output rate. On Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol that is 1,500 times $2 per million, or $0.003, plus 2,000 times $10 per million, or $0.02, for $0.023 per article and $4.60 for 200. The older GPT-4o at $2.50 input adds $0.00375 for $0.02375 per article, or $4.75. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at its peak rate of $0.30 and $1.20 costs $0.00045 plus $0.0024, or $0.00285 per article and $0.57 for 200. These are formulas applied to published rates, not measured bills. We have not measured latency for this page, because response time depends on model, load and output length, so time your own prompts. APIs bill each token as used with no monthly overage fee, but providers apply rate limits and spending caps, and cached input is cheaper than fresh input. To test this on your own numbers, run ten real requests, read the billed input and output tokens in the provider's usage dashboard, and compare them with the assumed token counts. If the real tokens differ by more than about 10 percent, change the assumption in Lab Settings, because token counts drive almost the whole result.
